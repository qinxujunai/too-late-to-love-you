来不及爱你

Too Late to Love You

After the breakup, she takes the ferry home with the only photograph of the two of them.

Why he made it

“Using AI to make a piece that says what you want to say is worth doing in itself.”

  1. The start

    A shot came first; the story came after.

    “There was one shot that turned out well, and I wanted to try telling a story with it. … Start from the first shot, then think about the story.”

  2. The title

    Learn to love yourself first, or it is too late to love anyone.

    “At first you may love someone with everything you have, and only later learn that to love someone, you first have to love yourself …”

    The title comes from the song 《来不及爱你》.

  3. The ending

    The ending gives no answer, only a door.

    “… in the end she walks out through that door. It stands for a new beginning, and leaves a blank.”

— Qin Xujun, edited from speech, translated from Chinese

How it was made

Seven shots, ten seconds each

Generated with the AI agent Muse. There was no script: the story grew forward from the first shot, generate, watch, reject, generate again. He edited and graded it himself.

  1. Shot 1 · Five attempts

    The cabin: she looks back

    It was the master shot, but its cold morning tones didn’t match the afternoon light after it, and she sat on the wrong side. Five approaches later, using its own frames as reference fixed it.

In a prompt, only what can be seen does any work

The whole film started from one English prompt. The part that worked: one continuous shot, one push-in, one clear end to the action (she looks back at the lens), wind and light doing the moving, no dialogue.

The part that did nothing was a string of adjectives: cinematic, moody, masterpiece, 4k. It asked for 4k; it came out at 720p.

After that, each shot described only action. Shot 2 said just this: she looks away, a tear falls, she stands, walks towards the camera, leaves the frame on the right, and the camera stays on the empty seat.

A little film grammar

“Once you can name the problem, it can be fixed.”

“You need some film basics: shot sizes, cuts, crossing the line, and the common language of the camera.” — Qin Xujun, translated from Chinese

Crossing the line

“The camera suddenly crosses an imaginary line of direction, so a person’s position or movement on screen flips, and the audience loses track of the space.”

— Qin Xujun, translated from Chinese

In this filmThe first take of shot 2 crossed the line when it jumped from the cabin to the deck; to him it “looked really fake”. After that one rule held for the whole film: she always moves left to right, leaving frame on the right and entering on the left.

  1. Shot size

    How much of the frame a person fills: wide, full, medium, close-up, extreme close-up. Change it and you change how close the audience is to her.

    In this filmThe opening pushes from a medium shot to a close-up so you get near her first. Shot 6 first cut straight to an aerial wide shot, too sudden; it became a pull-back from her face, step by step, until the whole sea is in view.

    Watch from 0:44 ›

  2. Matching action

    An action left unfinished in one shot is finished in the next. When it matches, the audience doesn’t notice the cut.

    In this filmEvery shot started from the real last frame of the one before, with text describing only what happens next. Describe the face, clothes or light again and the picture drifts.

    Watch from 0:11 ›

  3. Carry the feeling across the cut

    Like the costume, the feeling has to match: if she is crying at the end of one shot, she is crying at the start of the next.

    In this filmIn the first take of shot 3 she stopped crying the moment she stepped out; the tears vanished at the cut. In the remake she is still crying at the door and only wipes them at the railing.

    Watch from 0:12 ›

  4. Consistent colour

    One film should look as if it was shot on one day in one place.

    In this filmShot 1 came out in teal-and-orange morning tones; everything after is afternoon sun. He graded it himself in CapCut against three checks: her face back to a pinkish beige, the seats back to a clean blue, the window frames back to white.

    Watch from 0:06 ›

  5. Props tell the story

    Everything in the frame says something. Get a prop wrong and the feeling is wrong.

    In this filmThe photograph in her hand began as an old picture of two elderly people; to him “the feeling was wrong”. A young couple made it a breakup; then their backs by the sea, because small faces inside a frame distort first.

    Watch from 0:21 ›

  6. Hold back at the peak

    Where the feeling is strongest, play it smallest.

    In this filmThe first take of shot 4 opened on loud crying, face to camera, and was rejected. In the final cut she just smiles once through her tears; in shot 5 it is one deep breath and her shoulders dropping.
