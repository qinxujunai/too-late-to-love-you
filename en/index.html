来不及爱你

Too Late to Love You

After the breakup, she takes the ferry home with the only photograph of the two of them.

Why he made it

“Using AI to make a piece that says what you want to say is worth doing in itself.”

  1. The start

    A shot came first; the story came after.

    “There was one shot that turned out well, and I wanted to try telling a story with it. … Start from the first shot, then think about the story.”

  2. The title

    The title comes from the song, 《来不及爱你》 (Too Late to Love You).

    “At first you may love someone with everything you have, and only later learn that to love someone, you first have to love yourself …”

  3. The ending

    She walks through a door. The film doesn’t say what lies beyond it.

    “… in the end she walks out through that door. It stands for a new beginning, and leaves a blank.”

— Qin Xujun, edited from speech, translated from Chinese

How it was made

Seven shots, ten seconds each

Generated with the AI agent Muse. There was no script: the story grew forward from the first shot, generate, watch, reject, generate again. He edited and graded it himself.

  1. Shot 1 · Five attempts

    The cabin: she looks back

    It was the master shot, but its cold morning tones didn’t match the afternoon light after it, and she sat on the wrong side. Five approaches later, using its own frames as reference fixed it.

  2. Shot 2 · Rejected twice

    The cabin: she gets up

    The first version reversed her direction and turned the light grey; the second overplayed the crying. The third started from the previous shot’s real last frame and described only action.

  3. Shot 3 · Rejected once

    The deck: to the railing

    In the first version she stopped crying the moment she stepped out. In the remake she is still crying at the door and only wipes her tears at the railing.

  4. Shot 4 · Rejected once

    The deck: the photograph

    The first version opened on loud crying, face to camera. Redone in profile and quiet: she looks at the photograph and smiles once through her tears. That is the film’s high point.

  5. Shot 5 · First take

    The deck: one breath

    The photograph to her chest, back into the pocket, a deep breath into the wind, shoulders dropping. Letting go is done by the body, not the face.

  6. Shot 6 · Rejected twice

    Pulling back to the sea

    It began as an aerial wide shot that came from nowhere. He redesigned it as a pull-back from her face; the second version smiled too soon, the third carried the tears over and took its time.

The first prompt, and how the writing changed

The whole film started from one English prompt. The part that worked: one continuous shot, one push-in, one clear end to the action (she looks back at the lens), wind and light doing the moving, no dialogue.

The part that did nothing was a string of adjectives: cinematic, moody, masterpiece, 4k. It asked for 4k; it came out at 720p.

After that, each shot described only action. Shot 2 said just this: she looks away, a tear falls, she stands, walks towards the camera, leaves the frame on the right, and the camera stays on the empty seat.

Show the original prompt
A cinematic 10–second video of a beautiful young Asian woman on a moving ferry, wearing a beige trench coat and a thick oversized plaid scarf. Natural sunlight streams through the cabin windows, reflecting off the water outside. The camera starts with a medium shot, showing the melancholic and moody atmosphere of the boat cabin with blurred passengers in the background. In the final seconds, she slowly turns her head to look over her shoulder directly at the camera with a profound gaze. Handheld camera feel, 4k, photorealistic, cinematic lighting, moody color grading, masterpiece.

If you want to make one

Six things this film taught

  1. 1

    One action per shot

    If it doesn’t fit, split it in two. Let wind, water and light do the rest of the moving.

  2. 2

    Start each shot from the last frame

    Use the previous shot’s last frame as the opening image and describe only what happens next. Describe the face, clothes or light again and the picture drifts.

  3. 3

    Carry the feeling across the cut

    If she was crying at the end of one shot, she is crying at the start of the next. Feeling can change within a shot, not vanish at a cut.

  4. 4

    No faces in the photograph

    Small faces inside the frame distort and age first. Use backs or blur.

  5. 5

    Pick references by what the person is doing

    A reference image brings its colour and its posture. If she is standing in it, she will stand up.

  6. 6

    Hold back at the peak

    A smile through tears, one deep breath: stronger than loud crying.

“Wherever something feels wrong or uncomfortable, you can change it.” — Qin Xujun
