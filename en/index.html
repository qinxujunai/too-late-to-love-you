The story

She does not leave the photograph at sea

After a separation, she takes the ferry home with the one photograph of them. She cries in the cabin, looks at the picture on deck, and smiles for a fleeting moment through her tears.

She neither tears it up nor throws it away. She puts it safely back, breathes into the sea wind, and leaves the ferry.

Synopsis and readings are edited from the creative archive. The story is not presented as the director’s autobiography.

Making the film

There was no complete script at the start

This sequence follows creative decisions, not playback order. Rejected clips are described from the written record; no missing before-images have been reconstructed.

  1. 1

    Ten seconds before a story

    Warm cabin light, wind and a glance came first. The story emerged through discussion and trials, eventually finding its direction: she is taking the ferry home.

    Watch this moment 00:00.80 ›
  2. 2

    One prop changes the relationship

    An elderly pair suggested the wrong story. Young faces in a small photo proved unstable. Figures seen from behind retained the relationship without demanding fragile facial detail.

    Watch this moment 00:21.50 ›
  3. 3

    Carry the direction and tears across the cut

    Resemblance was not enough. Screen direction, rising movement and emotional state also had to match. The next prompt described action, leaving identity, clothes and light to the actual reference frame.

    Watch this moment 00:11.20 ›
  4. 4

    Even the favourite opening needed rethinking

    Five approaches were tried. A standing reference matched the face and light but also made her stand when she needed to sit. The solution returned to the opening’s own frames and carefully adjusted the reference light.

    Watch this moment 00:07.50 ›
  5. 5

    A beautiful aerial view can still be wrong

    A spectacular wide shot lacked a reason to leave her face. Pulling back in stages rebuilt the relationship between her, the ferry and the sea without restarting her emotion.

    Watch this moment 00:44.80 ›
  6. 6

    Do not turn the unknown into an answer

    The prettier walkway was set aside. The doorway mattered because its other side stayed unknown. The generated clips became a film through choosing actions, pauses and music, not by keeping every second.

Continuity is more than a matching face

Actual frames on either side of a cut in this film. These are not before-and-after revisions.

The shot size changes. The coat, scarf, cabin and crying state still need to belong to one moment. Reference frames constrain generation; direction, action, emotion and light still need to be checked in context.

The prompt that started it

Establish a person and place, one camera move and one endpoint. Let wind, water and light carry the surrounding motion.

Read the original English prompt
A cinematic 10–second video of a beautiful young Asian woman on a moving ferry, wearing a beige trench coat and a thick oversized plaid scarf. Natural sunlight streams through the cabin windows, reflecting off the water outside. The camera starts with a medium shot, showing the melancholic and moody atmosphere of the boat cabin with blurred passengers in the background. The camera slowly pushes in for an extreme, highly detailed close–up of the woman's face. The wind gently blows her messy hair across her face, highlighting her melancholic, deep, and thoughtful eyes. No speaking. In the final seconds, she slowly turns her head to look over her shoulder directly at the camera with a profound gaze. Handheld camera feel, 4k, photorealistic, cinematic lighting, moody color grading, masterpiece.
